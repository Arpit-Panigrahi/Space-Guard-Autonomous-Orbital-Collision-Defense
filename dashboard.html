Zero-to-Hero Guide

How Space-Guard Works

Four stages take us from raw satellite data to a precise collision probability and an evasive burn command.

Stage 01 / 04
Orbit Tracking — TLEs

We source Two-Line Element sets (TLEs) from CelesTrak for every tracked object in orbit. Each TLE is a compact two-line encoding of a satellite's orbital parameters at a reference epoch. We propagate them forward using the SGP4 model — a physics-based ballistic propagator accurate to ~1 km over 24 hours. Think of it as a train schedule for space: each satellite follows a predictable path unless forces like drag alter it.

Stage 02 / 04
Conjunction Screening

The screening engine evaluates all object pairs over a 24–72 hour lookahead window. When the predicted separation drops below 25 km, we use scipy.optimize.minimize_scalar to precisely locate the Time of Closest Approach (TCA) and compute the exact miss distance in kilometres. This two-stage approach (coarse grid then fine optimisation) is efficient enough to screen thousands of pairs in seconds.

Stage 03 / 04
Probability of Collision — Pc

A small miss distance is necessary but not sufficient for alarm — TLEs carry positional uncertainty (σ≈500 m). We model this in the B-plane using an Analytic 2D Isotropic Gaussian integral (the Foster/Alfano formulation used by ESA and NASA). The result is Pc — the exact probability the two hard-body spheres overlap. Pc > 1×10⁻⁴ triggers a Critical alert; >10⁻⁵ is High.

Stage 04 / 04
Evasive Maneuver Planning — CW

For Critical/High events, the Clohessy-Wiltshire State Transition Matrix computes the optimal impulsive burn. We decompose the ΔV budget into radial (R), along-track (T), and cross-track (N) components using SVD to find the burn direction that maximises miss distance shift. Critical insight: a 1 m/s burn 24h before TCA creates ~14× more separation than the same burn 1h before TCA — fuel efficiency collapses near TCA.

Historical Mode — Amber Alert

Iridium 33 & Cosmos 2251

On February 10, 2009, two satellites collided at 789 km altitude over Siberia at 11.7 km/s — the first accidental hypervelocity collision in history. We verify that Space-Guard, given only the pre-collision TLEs, would have raised a Critical alert and recommended a maneuver ~48 hours in advance.

2009-02-08 · T minus 48 hours
TLE Ingestion
Space-Guard ingests the historical TLEs for IRIDIUM 33 (NORAD 24946, epoch 09041) and COSMOS 2251 (NORAD 22675). These are the actual pre-collision element sets archived from the Space Surveillance Network.
2009-02-08 · 01:00 UTC
SGP4 Propagation & Screening
The conjunction engine sweeps a 2-hour window around the predicted pass using 60-second steps, then minimises the separation curve. It identifies a local minimum converging near 789 km altitude over the Taymyr Peninsula, Siberia.
2009-02-08 · 01:05 UTC
Analytic Pc Calculation — CRITICAL
The Foster/Alfano 2D Gaussian model computes Pc ≈ 1.94×10⁻⁴. This exceeds the Critical threshold of 1×10⁻⁴. The system immediately generates a maneuver recommendation with a 24-hour urgency window.
2009-02-10 · 16:56 UTC — COLLISION
Actual Impact
IRIDIUM 33 and COSMOS 2251 collide, generating ~2,300 trackable debris fragments and an estimated 100,000+ pieces ≥1 mm. The debris fields remain in orbit to this day, elevating collision risk for all LEO satellites.
